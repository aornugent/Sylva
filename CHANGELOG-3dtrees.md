# 3dtrees changes to Sylva

`main` mirrors upstream; `develop` is ours (`git log main..develop`). Newest first.

- `ScanIndex` keeps each pulse's number of returns (`las_number_of_returns`, 1 B per pulse) and says when the export deleted some of its echoes (`lost_echoes`); `qsm.pairs` reports it per row (`lost`), and `joint_table` leaves out passes by such pulses, since the deleted echo may have been their stop (TERN's exports delete about 10 % of a tree's passes this way). No output changes for stores without the attribute; the joint table changes on stores with it.
- `qsm.joint_fit` (Python `_core.qsm_joint_fit`) fits the joint wood, foliage and ray model end to end: a Gaussian Markov prior over the tree, per-(scan, branch) nuisance offsets, continuation over the surface kernel, damped saddle-free Newton, and the Laplace summary (sds, data shares). The Newton system is a tree of dense branch blocks (every likelihood term lies within a branch; the prior links a branch to its parent row), solved exactly by eliminating branches into their parents, leaves first, with no fill and no new dependency; the same tree gives the posterior covariance top-down. `qsm.joint::joint_table` (`_core.qsm_joint_table`) builds the model's pair table from the enumeration. 3dtrees' Python fit, now its oracle, agrees step for step; a 416-row tree on 850k pairs fits in 47 s (minutes in Python). Additive.
- `qsm.joint` evaluates its likelihood generically: plain values for the objective (no dual arithmetic it doesn't need) and dual numbers for the Newton blocks, from one source. Python holds the pairs in a `_core.JointTable` for the whole fit and passes per-row states and nuisance offsets, which Rust gathers per pair. Block sums are deterministic (pairs sorted by group, fixed chunks summed in order): the old parallel fold added in a different order each run, and the joint fit on a real tree followed that rounding to different optima (0.57-0.67 x RCT across identical runs). About 2.5x faster in 3dtrees' fits. The old `qsm_joint_loglik`/`qsm_joint_blocks` functions are replaced by the class.
- `qsm.pairs` takes a tube radius per cylinder (one for all still works from Python), and shares the closest-approach geometry with `evidence`; the joint binding takes named columns and Rust holds one `Pulse` per pair. A caller with cylinders of different sizes had to run `pairs` at the widest radius and drop again the pulses that stopped short of each cylinder's own tube, which 3dtrees got wrong once. No output changes for one radius.
- `qsm.joint` (Rust; Python `_core.qsm_joint_loglik` / `qsm_joint_blocks`): the per-pulse likelihood of 3dtrees' joint wood, foliage and ray model with its exact gradient and Hessian, from one evaluation on second-order dual numbers, summed by group in parallel. It replaces numpy finite differences that were 8x slower and off by 1-38 % on its needle-sharp likelihood; the model is still settling in 3dtrees, so this is its hot path only, behind a private binding. Adds a `libm` dependency (already in the lock) for erfc. Additive: no output changes.
- `synthetic.scan(..., solids=)` puts solid, opaque capped cylinders in a beam scan beside the points; a sub-beam stops at their surface and the echo takes the attributes of a point that stands for them. Wood drawn as surface points is only as closed, and as thin, as its patches: at practical densities pulses echo off the back face, and patches laid tangent widen thin wood's silhouette by up to 60 %, so the 3dtrees bench couldn't score wood under 2 cm (3dtrees research 74). Scans without solids are unchanged.
- `qsm.pairs` returns the raw table `qsm.evidence` reduces inside: every pulse near each cylinder that reached its disc, with its closest approach to the axis and all its echoes (range, reflectance, deviation). The joint wood, foliage and ray model (3dtrees research 74) is a per-pulse likelihood and needs that table, not evidence's summaries. `ScanIndex.origin` exposes the scanner position. Additive: no output changes.
- Synced to upstream v0.3.2 and dropped our `Shots.from_scan`. Upstream's `fill_missing_from_timing` now recovers misses from pulse timing, the one part of `from_scan` that needed Sylva; 3dtrees sorts the export itself and calls `from_pointcloud`.
- `qsm.evidence` checks each cylinder of a QSM against the scan rays: the radius its echoes measure and how precisely, the beam calliper, the pulses that pass inside the claimed radius, and how far pulses penetrate (with the hits' reflectance and deviation). RCT's radii are mostly a taper prior rather than measurements, and this says which ones the rays support, contradict or can't see, and tells leaf clumps fitted as fat twigs from wood. `qsm.ScanIndex` bins each scan's pulses by direction so a whole tile's cylinders can be checked in seconds.
- `io.tile_ray_clouds` cuts Shots files into buffered raycloudtools ray-cloud tiles, streamed with bounded memory. `rayextract` can't fit a whole plot in memory, and its input has to keep each echo's ray back to its scanner.
- `qsm.surface_support` and `qsm.surface_distance` measure how well points sit on a QSM's cylinder surfaces. They were the first screen for bad fits; `evidence` has since replaced `surface_support`, and the 3dtrees viewer still colours points with `surface_distance`.
