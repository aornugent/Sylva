# 3dtrees changes to Sylva

What our `develop` adds over upstream (`git log main..develop`), as it stands: rewritten as `develop` changes, until we version. `main` mirrors upstream.

- `qsm.support_tree` doesn't use a piece's own circle that reaches the edge of the tube its pulses were taken in, nor the lower bound of the woody run it spanned (3dtrees ticket 109): beyond the tube the evidence is cut off, and such a circle was a stem captured by a neighbour's or the ground's mass, or one drifted onto a leaf clump (3dtrees research 106). The piece is bounded instead, and `TUBE_EDGE` says why. Changes `support_tree`'s outputs on those pieces.
- `qsm.support_tree` takes the point evidence (3dtrees ticket 109): a row none of whose pieces has a circle of its own gets one fitted to the stops in its tubes, as 3dtrees' refit fitted RCT's points (RANSAC and its tests), entered as a measurement of its pieces' centre and R (sd 0.136 in log R) beside their bounds, unless the ray veto refutes it, from the stops nearer its own claimed axis than any other the tree or its context claims (as the refit took each point to its nearest cylinder); its nodes' `basis` is `POINTS` (new; the codes renumber), it reports `r_points` and the volume's `points` share. We need it because the refit that measured these rows is gone, and they carried most of the plot's uncertainty: on research 106's 80 top-variance trees the summed volume variance falls 41 % for +0.06 CPU-s per tree. Changes `support_tree`'s outputs (`JointParams` version 10).
- `qsm.support_tree` becomes 3dtrees' measurement (ticket 105): passes through a claim bound its radius (the ray veto) and a posterior is held inside its own bounds, so what is served isn't refuted; the site's stem D/H (`dh`) caps unmeasured radii after the solve; each node says how its radius is known (`basis`, `diag`), each node's sd is widened by its basis's calibrated factor (`WIDEN`: 2.1 measured, 2.1 points, 3.6 bounded, 3.1 prior, 2.3 capped, each covering 90 % of Alice Mulga's refit circles on held-out trees; not a `JointParams` field), a row's radius is its pieces' root mean square (its volume theirs) and each tree gets its volume's mean (the volume of record, split by basis), its interval and the drawn geometry's point from its joint posterior. Changes `support_tree`'s outputs; `joint_tree`'s and `joint_fit`'s fits are unchanged.
- `qsm.support_tree` says where its radii are not determined, and why (research 93 §20). An exact stem whose sides, as its scans see them (the pulses that reach each edge's neighbourhood and its front against those that echo further in front, read with `ScanIndex::ranges`, in Rust), leave its radius trading against its centre (one edge and the front from one scan, the other edge behind a crossing branch) says nothing along that ridge, and its own bounds (its front arc's span, the capacity floor) enter the tree beside it: its radius is the prior's cut by them. An edge past the fit's table isn't judged. Every row and node gets a diagnostic word `diag` (`SupportDiag`: ONE_SIDED, SHADOWED, SUB_FOOTPRINT, TUBE_EDGE, CONTESTED) and three numbers: `data_share` (the share of log R's posterior precision from its own data), `contested` (the share of its stops another node or the context claims) and `deleted` (the share of its pulses with deleted echoes). We need them so a reviewer or the screen can act on what the fit can't decide. Changes outputs: the radii of shadowed exact stems and the tree around them (8 of 265 exact stems on the 3dtrees bench); measured and two-sided rows unchanged.
- `qsm.support_tree` fits its routed pieces by the exact likelihood (research 93 §18, `support_tree/routed.rs`). Seeds come from each scan's runs, found slice by slice along the piece, each measured as a circle with its own centre, lean and radius and scored by every scan's woody stops on its front arc (hulls dropped by their opacity; no seed, no fit). The piece's pulses are taken once, with the tree's measured and bounded pieces owning the stops they explain better, each seed seeing them from its own leaning axis (never the hull's lean, which on tree 849 row 4 joined two stems); every pulse is fitted on pieces of at most 8,000 pairs, and one in `ceil(n / 8000)` above that (which ones by a hash of the pulse's direction and `JointParams.thin_seed`, 0 in production, so a scan cut to a tree's pulses fits the same ones and a bench can vary the choice); then 1, 2, ... stems by Newton to its stopping rule while the fits converge and BIC on the same pulses rises, and a stem whose message isn't finite or says nothing of its radius steps the ladder back to the fit of one stem fewer. Each stem's likelihood message (foliage integrated out), its information scaled to all the piece's pairs and discounted by their correlation (`exist_scale`) with `model_sd` added to its variances, measures the piece's centre and R in the tree, the lean its seed's. Of the stems starting near the parent's end (within the larger radius and 2 cm), the one whose message says most of its radius continues the piece (the nearest where none is that near); another continues a matching stem of a routed parent piece or is a node beside it (`pieces` `src`, `stem`; per piece `k_stems`, `split_gain`, `n_exact`, `evals_value`, `evals_deriv`). A fit is refused, and the piece is then the prior cut by its upper bound and floor, where there is no seed or no pulse, Newton didn't converge, no rung of the ladder has every stem finite and saying something of its radius (sd of log R at most 1), or its one stem is narrower than the piece's detection bound; `refused` says which, per piece (`SupportRefusal`). Every stop is classified (`echoes`, `EchoPath`), keyed by scan, the pulse's row in its Shots file and the echo's place in its pulse, as the tile sidecars are: by its piece's circle's front arc times its echo's weight, or on a routed piece with a fit by the exact likelihood's wood and leaf terms; `joint_tree` gains the same `echoes`. Each routed stem's foliage rate is reported (`pieces.lam`). It exists because the routed pieces hold most of a plot's thick multi-stem and leaning wood. On 3dtrees research 93's bench (Alice Mulga: 20 random trees, 849, 432 and 676; each number over three thinning seeds) the plot extrapolates to about 0.36-0.38 CPU-s per tree (target 0.45; tree 849 3.3 CPU-s on one thread against `joint_tree`'s ~290). On the refit's real circles on routed and multi-stem rows it is 0.067-0.083 in log R over seeds (the support path alone 0.13, RCT 0.26), and on tree 849's 12 scored circles 0.072-0.079 (`joint_tree` 0.073). k changes between thinning seeds on 3 of 32 multi-stem rows (16 when half of every piece was fitted), all on pieces over the pair budget. Changes `support_tree`'s outputs; `joint_tree`'s and `joint_fit`'s fits are unchanged.
- `qsm.support_tree`'s tree solve: where a piece's cavity lies far from its bounds (the marginal's quadrature grid misses the tilted mass), its moments come from a grid across the cavity and the bounds, refined about the peak; a site or fork weight whose step turns back is damped harder and grows back to a full step while it doesn't; it has converged when no step would move by more than 1e-3 sd undamped; the final solve's `rounds` are reported. Of the 7 trees whose solve didn't converge in the plot run, all 7 now do (tree 2776, 7.6k rows: 150 rounds, the whole tree 3.9 CPU-s on one thread against 7.4). Changes `support_tree`'s outputs slightly.
- `qsm.support_tree` (with `SupportFlag`) is the joint model's fast path for one tree. A row longer than 30 cm is cut into pieces of at most 30 cm, and each piece is a node of the tree, so a bent row is a chain of straight pieces (outputs per row and per piece, under `pieces`). Each piece's pulses, per scan, reduce to a profile across it whose edges and front depth measure the support function of its cross-section, linear in a leaning circle's centre, lean and radius; a stop counts as wood by its echo character (`JointParams.refl_*`, thin wood darker by `refl_thin`). Where a circle is measured it enters the tree as a Gaussian summary. Where none is determined, each scan still bounds R per slice along the piece (medians of the woody run's width, and of the gap between the passes beside it or, where no woody stop is, either side of the woody centre; a side with nothing recorded gives no bound), and wood that was hit fills at least `tau` of the footprint; these enter as censored terms on log R, solved by expectation propagation with one site per piece (its tilted moments by quadrature), so the radius is the prior cut by the bounds (`r_lo`, `r_hi`, and the capacity floor apart as `r_floor`; `converged` says whether the solve settled). Each piece also gets an existence log odds from its core's echo character and opacity (a stop for wood, a pass against it, the passes' net at most one sure echo), each scan's capped and weighted (`stop_wood`, `stop_leaf`, `exist_scale`), and a chain along the branches (`exist`, `exist_off`) gives `p_wood`; these are fitted on `joint_tree`'s wood and NO_WOOD rows of four trees (held-out AUC 0.68-0.92). The prior is after `joint_tree`'s: the taper's steps and the base's level, at a fork the child's share of the subtree wood length (not of the prior area) as a Student-t term (2 degrees of freedom, reweighted inside the solve) so a small shoot measured fat can't inflate its parent, the centre's continuity along a branch and the lean's random walk between a row's pieces (`lean_bend`), the centre's anchor and a weak lean anchor (`lean_sd`), no tip anchor. Pieces with two or more stems in a profile (`RUNS`) or a hull or misfit within the piece (`HULL`) are routed to the exact likelihood (the bullet above); `BOUNDS` and `POROUS` are resolved here. It exists because `joint_tree` takes minutes per large tree and the plot needs about half a CPU-second per tree: without the exact fits it extrapolates to 0.18 CPU-s per tree on Alice Mulga, and on the refit's real circles it is as good as `joint_tree` where it measures (tree 849 0.062 against 0.073). Its bounds are mostly weak: on the 20 trees 11 % of bounded pieces have an upper bound and 13 % a lower one above the floor. `JointParams` gains the `refl_*` fields, `refl_thin`, `stop_wood`, `stop_leaf`, `exist_scale`, `model_sd`, `lean_sd`, `lean_bend`, `exist`, `exist_off` and `thin_seed` and goes to version 8 (earlier records are refused); `joint_tree`'s and `joint_fit`'s fits are unchanged. Changes `support_tree`'s outputs.
- `develop` is upstream `main` (v0.3.2 and its Zenodo DOI) without `Shots.from_scan`, which 3dtrees replaced: upstream's `fill_missing_from_timing` recovers misses from pulse timing, so 3dtrees sorts each export itself and calls `from_pointcloud`. Changes no outputs.
- `qsm.evidence` checks each cylinder of a QSM against the scan rays: the radius its echoes measure and how precisely, the beam calliper, the pulses that pass inside the claimed radius, and how far pulses penetrate, with the hits' reflectance and deviation. RCT's radii are mostly a taper prior, and this says which ones the rays support, contradict or can't see, and tells leaf clumps fitted as fat twigs from wood. `qsm.ScanIndex` bins each scan's pulses by direction, so a tile's cylinders are checked in seconds, and keeps each echo's return number and each pulse's number of returns where the store has them, and each pulse's row in its Shots file (4 B per pulse, in Rust `ScanIndex::file_row`), so fits' echoes join to the tile sidecars' echoes. Additive.
- `qsm.pairs` returns the raw table that `qsm.evidence` reduces inside: every pulse near each cylinder (a tube radius per cylinder, or one for all) that reached its tube, with its closest approach to the axis, all its echoes (range, reflectance, deviation, return number), its number of returns and whether the export deleted some of its echoes (`lost`); `ScanIndex.origin` gives the scanner position. The joint model is a per-pulse likelihood and needs that table, not evidence's summaries. Additive.
- `qsm.joint_tree` (with `JointParams` and `JointReason`) fits the joint wood, foliage and ray model to one tree in one call: its rows and its neighbours' cylinders in, a fitted row per stem out, with each row's radius interval, posterior shares, existence (`log_odds`, `p_wood`, `reason`) and the split of clumps into solid stems, decided per row by evidence against a BIC price (`split_gain`). Each row is fitted alone first, then the tree with per-row and per-branch step control; each stop is owned by the row whose surface explains it. 3dtrees' screen and candidates call it per tree, as they call `evidence`. It also returns its stops classified (`echoes`, as `support_tree`'s: the softmax of each stop's wood and leaf terms at its row's fit); its fit is bit-identical to before. `JointParams` is checked (its version, exactly its fields) and is what a cache of fits keys on. A new function: changes no existing output.
- `qsm.joint_fit` and `qsm.joint_table` are `joint_tree`'s parts, for research: the pair table (passes where a deleted echo could lie in the chord are kept and flagged `deleted`, each echo the export deleted placed between its recorded neighbours by return number), and the fit of a table, saddle-free Newton with a projected line search over a Gaussian Markov prior (taper, soft Leonardo at forks, a tip anchor, the centre's and foliage's random walks, per-(scan, branch) offsets) and an optional radius cap per row. The Newton system is solved exactly as a tree of branch blocks, leaves first, and block sums are deterministic. It returns the Laplace summary (from the true Hessian where it is negative definite: `exact`), each row's share of its own rays (`rho_local`), its likelihood message, an η profile (in Rust optional: `FitOptions.profile`) and (in Rust) its pair evaluations (`Fit.evals`). Additive.
- `qsm.joint` is the joint model's per-pulse likelihood in Rust, with its exact gradient and Hessian from one evaluation on dual numbers; `_core.qsm_joint_loglik` exposes it, stateless, for checking. The likelihood needs wood wide enough to give the share τ of the footprint, with a detection edge of sd `edge` and mixed pixels; `hide` makes a pass by a pulse that lost an echo a mixture with a deleted stop. It adds a `libm` dependency (already in the lock) for erfc. Additive.
- Parity cases for the joint model's likelihood and pair table (`tests/parity/cases_joint.py`), recorded against 3dtrees' numpy oracle, so a change to either is a recorded change. Tests only.
- `synthetic.scan(..., solids=)` puts solid, opaque capped cylinders in a beam scan beside the points: a sub-beam stops at their surface and its echo takes the attributes of a point that stands for them. Wood drawn as surface points echoes off its back face and has its thin silhouette widened by up to 60 %, so the 3dtrees bench couldn't score wood under 2 cm. Scans without solids are unchanged.
- `io.tile_ray_clouds` cuts Shots files into buffered raycloudtools ray-cloud tiles, streamed with bounded memory. `rayextract` can't fit a whole plot in memory, and its input has to keep each echo's ray back to its scanner. Beside each cloud, sidecars in cloud order name every ray's echo: `scan.u16` (its file), `pulse.u32` (its pulse's row) and `echo.u8` (its place in the pulse), so what `rayextract` writes per ray joins to the echo and its pulse exactly. `cloud.ply` is byte-identical, but the tiles are keyed on `ray_tiles.rs`, so each plot re-tiles once, rebuilds its RCT graphs cold and rescreens; verdicts carry over, since they key on structure.
- `qsm.surface_support` and `qsm.surface_distance` measure how well points sit on a QSM's cylinder surfaces; the 3dtrees viewer colours points with `surface_distance`. Additive.
- Docstrings for `qsm.evidence.contradicted`, `qsm.surface_distance` and `ScanIndex.n_pulses`, so upstream's `test_public_api_is_documented` passes on `develop`. Docs only.
